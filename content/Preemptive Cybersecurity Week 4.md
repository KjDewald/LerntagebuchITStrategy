

### What did I do?

I had taken a course in a previous degree on cybersecurity and was interested to see how a preemptive approach would look like.  
From what I understand, pre-emptive security could be as simple as training employees correctly and updating systems regularly. However in my opinion this is much closer to prevention, which should be part of any security concept.

### What did I learn?

I learned that preemptive cybersecurity is much more than simple prevention. It is also about raising the stakes for hackers (i.e. through honeypots) and deterring from an attack.  
While prevention might focus more on the client side, pre-emptive security is more comprehensive and holistic focusing also on the adversaries side.  
Unfortunately, a lot of the measures were not explained - how does a moving target security work? What are dynamic security protocols and how are they applied without confusing the employees?  
Also I was a bit confused by the deceive idea - as far as I understood previously, security should not rely on obscurity, it should be mechanically sound no matter how much the adversary knows about the system. I don't know if this only applies to cryptography or is a comprehensive idea - [Kerckhoff's principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerckhoffs%27s_principle).
