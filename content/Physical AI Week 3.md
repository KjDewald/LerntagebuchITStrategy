

### What did I do?

I looked up the concept of Physical Ai beforehand, as with the [[Computer Vision & AI Week 2|Computer Vision]] Presentation in the last week, I had trouble imagining how the topic might fit into the presentation.  
As such I restricted myself to a broad understanding of what is considered Physical AI and how it can already be implemented. From a very general point of view I thought that the [[Self Driving Vehicles Week 1|self driving vehicles]] from the first week could also be considered a representation of physical ai. Even the [[Computer Vision & AI Week 2|Computer Vision]] presentation could (in parts) be considered a type of physical ai - at least according to the [Gabler](https://wirtschaftslexikon.gabler.de/definition/physical-ai-177399) definition that broadly states that Physical AI is the connection of artificial intelligence with physical systems such as cameras, drones, robots.  
As with the [[Domain Specific Language Models Week 2|DSLMs]] there wasn't really a good Wikipedia introduction to the topic.

### What did I learn?

I learned that the definition I had originally read might not be super accurate.  
Apparently there are a bunch (like 6) of different things that need to be present so that something can be considered physical AI. I liked the examples in the presentation that drove that point home.
