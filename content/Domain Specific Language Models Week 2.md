

### What did I do?

As with all the other topics I researched Domain Specific Language Models a bit previous to the presentation, so that I could follow the content.  
Contrary to the other presentations however, there was no Wikipedia Page to get an overview of the topic. To me, that was a first sign, that the topic is quite new and that there may also not yet be a very solid shared understanding of it.  
From the explanation of [this IBM page](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/domain-specific-llm), I understand what the advantages of Domain Specific Language Models are. Training the LLM on a specific Domain and handpicking the resources it should be trained on, eliminates most of the issues that industries currently encounter.  
My brother, a lawyer, told me that his law offices also uses domain specific LLMs and has been for quite some time. For routine tasks, these LLMs can be super helpful and cut down on much of the time needed to study cases and create basic write-ups.  
At the same time, I could imagine that DSLMs also add to a deskilling issue - if I have no need to get to know the detailed, nitty gritty of my domain (because the LLM does that for me), I would imagine that dependency increases while critical thinking decreases.  
I wonder if that is an issue for companies as well?

### What did I learn?

I would have assumed that DSLMs are safer than general LLMs, especially when it comes to hallucinations. However, after the presentation I can see that DSLMs may be generally more accurate, but due to higher trust placed in these specialized systems, errors are even less likely to be discovered.  
Over-trust seems to be the biggest issue in this field. When DSLMs are good enough to work in 99% of the cases, the 1% is quickly overlooked and difficult to catch - in extreme situations, as we were tasked to consider in the exercise, this could end in dramatic losses to the company.  
I am unsure if there are any ways to mitigate these issues without running into yet another over-trust issue.  
Any measure would make employees trust more in the system. Meanwhile the system might never be a 100% correct.

### Self-Reflection

I thought this was a really interesting topic, because it related in its core problem to mine. As with DSLMs trust is central for Agentic User Interfaces. Unlike DSLMs, Agentic User Interfaces do not struggle with too much trust, but rather too little. Maybe the two work best in combination?
