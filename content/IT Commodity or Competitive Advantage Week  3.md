I researched a little bit why this is even a question. In my opinion, recent developments have shown that IT remains a competitive advantage if it manages to introduce a paradigm shift. That's exactly what happened with AI, as I see it. Now, that AI has become ubiquitous I can see the point of calling it or IT a commodity. ### What did I learn?

I think the answer I arrived at previous to the presentation holds. Since the question is not If a company uses IT, the core point of discussion is HOW it uses IT (since whether or not is not a question in this day and age). This in turn is also a great motivator for the [[Digitalization, Automation and IT Strategy Week 3|IT Strategy Presentation]]  
that would come later that same day.  
Another important point is that not every common IT tool is necessary. As currently demonstrated by a number of companies that invest in chatbots. Competitive Advantage can also come from abstaining from IT (or certain aspects of it) - by focusing on what is relevant, costs can be cut, and money can be invested in the core product of a company.
